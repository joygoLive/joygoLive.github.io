LOCI · Method of Loci

A room you can walk your memory through.

Objects sit in places. You look around, then answer what was where. The 3D is not decoration — the method of loci has been spatial since antiquity, so the room is the method itself.

Try a room What we measured

It is also a testbed. A room is one JSON file, four interchangeable runtimes draw the same scene, and every session reports frames, jank, input lag and load time from the device that ran it.

In the making at joygoLive. Open it on the device you want to see it on.
Premise

Serving 3D content on the web

The question was not whether a browser can draw a room. It can. The question is what it costs to author one, hand it to development, put it in front of a phone, and find out what that phone did with it. LOCI exists to run that loop once, end to end.

Content outside the codeIf a new room needs a developer, planning does not own the content. A room is one file, and the folder is the catalogue.
The runtime is a choice, not a foundationAssets and content sit outside the renderer, so swapping engines changes one module. Four already do.
The device answers, not the spec sheetEvery session sends back what actually happened on it. Guesses about phone performance were wrong twice already.
Scope

What is built

A room is a box with furniture, places on the furniture, and objects on the places. Everything about it — size, light, furniture, places, objects, learning time, price tier — lives in one JSON file that planning edits in a visual editor with the same renderer beside it.

  • One contract, three readers. The validation rules live in a single file used by the editor, the command line, and the page itself. Two copies drift, and then something passes review and breaks on screen.
  • Rules that are conditions, not taste. Places must differ in height, or they stop being distinct places and nothing attaches. Two items may not share a word, or scoring has no answer. A training room may not exceed sixteen places, because past that no one is memorising anything.
  • Four runtimes, one scene. Raw WebGL2, three.js, raw WebGPU and Canvas 2D draw the same room from the same bytes. The library is fetched only when chosen, so the transfer comparison stays honest.
  • Nothing ships without review. Planning uploads, validation gates it, and it waits until development approves. Uploads, rejections and approvals are appended to a ledger everyone reads.
Pipeline

Authoring to operations, once through

Four stages, each with something that stops the next one from inheriting a mistake.

01
Authoring

A form with the live scene next to it. Furniture comes from presets whose positions are computed against the room size, and a place snaps onto a surface rather than being typed as coordinates.

Gate: validation runs while you type. Failing means the upload button will not press.
02
Handoff

Upload puts the room in review, not in front of users. Development opens it at a staged address that requires a login, and either approves or rejects with a reason planning reads.

Gate: validation runs again at approval. What passed an hour ago is not what is being published now.
03
Serving

Static files, no install, no store. The user picks the room and the runtime on the first screen. Everything is precompressed with brotli and served that way.

Gate: the server serves an allow-list. Anything not named is a 404, including the directory listing.
04
Operations

Each session reports frames, worst 1%, jank, input lag, thermal drift, load breakdown and per-place results, split by runtime. Feedback is left on the section it is about, and acceptance is decided and recorded.

Gate: the ledger is append-only. There is no code path that deletes from it.
Measured

What the phone said

One iPhone, one stress room: 40 places, 80 pieces of furniture, 2.46 million triangles. Every runtime drew it, and the numbers came back from the device.

iPhone 14 Pro / 15 / 16 class, viewport 1179 × 1995, over the public address. Library size is what crossed the wire, brotli compressed.

  • Runtime choice is not a frame-rate decision. Draw calls varied by a factor of thirty-four and the median frame time stayed at 17 ms. Even without a 3D pipeline at all, Canvas 2D held the same rate.
  • The only axis that separated them was loading, and three.js was twice as slow because of its library — the one place where picking a runtime shows up for the user.
  • Compression did not fix loading. Precompressing the mesh took it from 365 KB to 73 KB, and time to first frame did not move. Transfer was not the bottleneck: parsing, shader compilation and GPU upload were. The assumption was wrong and the measurement said so.
  • The first run costs more than the second. Same device, same room, same runtime: 22 ms and 24 ms of input lag on the first pass, 17 ms and 12 ms on the next. Shader compilation and cold caches are paid once, by the person opening it for the first time.
Limits

What this does not claim

  • No claim that it improves memory. The method of loci is a technique, and LOCI is a place to practise it. Nothing here has been trialled, and no cognitive, medical or preventive benefit is asserted.
  • The sample is two devices. One phone and one laptop. Read it as direction, not as population statistics.
  • Unity and Unreal are not measured. Unity needs its editor to build and has not been attached; Unreal has had no official web target since 4.24. Their figures on the admin side are published estimates and are labelled as such.
  • Payment is a seam, not a feature. Tier and price are in the contract and the operations view shows the columns, but nothing charges anything and the numbers are simulated.
  • Assets that reach a browser cannot be protected. Anything drawn has to arrive. What can be done is gate it before sending, watermark to trace a leak, and keep the value in what does not travel.
