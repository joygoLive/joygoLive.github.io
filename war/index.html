Augmented reality that opens from a link.

Three demos in one page: a real-size object placed on your floor, 3D standing on printed matter, and a face tracked in real time. Nothing is installed and no account is made — the camera opens from a link and every frame stays on the device.

Design

Three decisions that shaped it

Web AR has been possible for years and expensive for just as long. What changed is the price of the engine, and that changed what could be built without a budget.

The engine became free in February

The world-tracking engine used here was a paid platform until early 2026, when it was released as open source and a free binary. That single change turned a demo that would have carried a monthly bill into one that costs nothing to run.

Configuration, not code

Which demos appear, what the objects are and how large, which image is the target, every line of on-screen text — all of it is a settings file. Adapting the demo to a different industry does not require touching the code.

The screen says what it cannot do

Each demo carries one line about its own boundary. A demo that hides its limits produces a plan that breaks on contact with them, which costs more than the demo was worth.

Demos

Three things a camera can do, and one that keeps them

Each demo proves one capability. Together they cover the ground most AR plans start from.

Place it in the room

Tap the floor and a real-size object stands there — a sofa, a massage chair, a 65-inch television, with its dimensions floating above it. Walk away and back and it has not moved; when it leaves the frame an arrow points to it and gives the distance.

Printed matter becomes the anchor

Point at the target card and 3D stands on it, following the paper as it tilts. You can also register your own printed matter on the spot: frame it, capture it, and the demo tells you within seconds whether it can be recognised.

A face, 478 points of it

The face surface is tracked in real time and worn items sit on it — glasses, a moustache — following expression and angle. The same tracking is what eyewear and cosmetics try-on runs on.

And a capture button on all three. The camera view and the 3D are saved as one image, so what a visitor tries does not vanish when they close the tab.

Limits

What this does not claim

It forgets where things were

Placement lasts for the session. Close the tab and the room is unknown again — yesterday's spot cannot be found. Printed matter is the way around that, because the paper carries the coordinates.

The picture is a preview, not a photograph

The browser receives a preview stream, not the multi-frame processing a camera app applies. The image is softer than the one the phone's own camera produces, and no engine changes that.

Old phones run it slowly

A 2026 iPhone holds 60 frames a second; a 2019 Android holds seven. The demo measures this and says so on screen rather than letting it read as a fault, and heavier demos can be switched off for such devices.

AR itself cannot be the product

The engine licence allows free use but not a paid service whose value comes mostly from the engine. AR has to be the method here — the value has to come from the content, the venue or the transaction.
