Design

Three decisions that shaped it

Food-and-music pairing has been tried several times. The earlier attempts either paired photos with songs and were absorbed by larger social apps, relied on curators and did not scale, or treated pairing as a formula and stopped being fun.

The unit is venue × dish × songA dish alone produces a single right answer, which is a recipe site. Tying each pairing to the place it was eaten turns the difference between places into the thing people compare.
Posted by guests, not curatedOnly someone who sat in that place can say what fits it. A pairing takes a photo, a venue, a dish and a song, and needs no account: the server names you after a dish.
Humor is a reaction, not a genreThree reactions: a perfect fit, a forced fit, and one that does not fit but is funny. The third keeps absurd pairings in the ranking instead of burying them.
Posting

Four fields, one of them optional

Posting has to be quicker than deciding not to. Each field offers what already exists before asking for anything new, so the same venue or dish does not split into several spellings.

A photo, if you want oneResized in the browser before upload, which also drops the location stored inside the photo.
A venue, or somewhere elseExisting venues in the neighborhood come first. Home, delivery, office and outdoors cover the pairings that happen away from any venue.
The dishSpacing and punctuation are ignored when dishes are compared, so every spelling of the same dish lands on the same page.
One song and one lineSearched in a public music catalog with a 30-second preview. The server looks the song up again by its id rather than trusting what the page sent.

Every pairing becomes a share card. A portrait image sized for stories, and a link that shows the photo and song in chat previews before anyone opens it.

Pages

Same dish, a different song per venue

A dish page lines venues up side by side. A venue page shows what its guests agreed on, but only once enough of them have posted.

VenueTheme song ranking, the funniest pick, one song per dish on the menuRanking after ten pairings; until then, a progress bar

Ten, not two. Declaring a venue's theme song from two posts would let the first guest define the place. The threshold is an operator setting, and a venue can be opened or held manually.

Moderation

Content posted by guests needs a floor

Most of this content happens over drinks. The rules are few and applied the same way every time, so moderation is a queue rather than a judgment call per post.

Adults only, confirmed onceBefore the first post: 19 or older and the house rules. Reading needs neither.
Three reports hide a postFrom three different people. The post stays hidden until an operator keeps it, hides it, or hides it and restricts the author.
Duplicate venues are suggested, not mergedNames that differ only by neighborhood or branch suffix are joined when posted. Names that merely contain one another are listed for an operator, since a second branch is a different venue.
Reactions reach you, at most hourlyWeb push, one notification per pairing per hour. Reactions arrive in bursts right after a share, and a phone that buzzes each time gets its notifications turned off.
Limits

What this does not claim

Listing what is out of scope is more useful than listing features, because most of it was left out on purpose.

No music is servedA 30-second preview from the catalog and links out to listen. No audio file is stored or streamed from here.
Not a review siteNo ratings, prices or opening hours. A venue is identified by its name and neighborhood only; there is no map yet.
One device, one identityNo sign-up means no account to move. Clearing the browser starts a new name; posts stay where they were.
Four neighborhoods firstSeongsu, Euljiro, Pangyo and Bundang (Sunae · Jeongja · Seohyeon), where a venue page can actually fill up. Spreading thin across a city leaves every page empty.
