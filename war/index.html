Augmented reality that opens from a link.

Four demos in one page: an object at its real size standing in the room, 3D standing on printed matter, a face tracked in real time, and any real object cut out of the view and stood up beside them. Design

Three decisions that shaped it

Web AR has been possible for years and expensive for just as long. What changed in early 2026 was the price of the engine, and that changed what could be built with no budget at all.

The engine stopped costing moneyWorld tracking in a browser — an object staying where you put it while you walk around it — came from one paid platform. In February 2026 it was released as open source with a free binary. A demo that would have carried a monthly bill now runs on static files, which is the only reason this exists.
Configuration, not codeWhich demos appear, what the objects are and how many centimetres, which image is the target, the sharpening strength, every line of on-screen text — all of it is one settings file behind an admin page. Fitting the demo to a different industry is editing values, not shipping a build.
The screen says what it cannot doEach demo carries one line about its own boundary, and the page you are reading ends with four more. A demo that hides its limits produces a plan that breaks on contact with them, and that costs more than the demo was ever worth.
Demos

Four things a camera can do, and one that keeps them

Each demo proves one capability, and each is switched on or off from the admin page. Together they cover the ground most AR plans start from.

Place it in the roomTap and a real-size object stands in that direction — a sofa, a massage chair, a 65-inch television — with its dimensions above it. Walk away and come back and it is still there, give or take the error that accumulates. When it leaves the frame an arrow points to it and gives the distance, which is the demonstration: the system still knows where it is.
Printed matter becomes the anchorPoint at the target card and 3D stands on it, tilting as the paper tilts. You can also register your own printed matter on the spot — frame it, capture it, and within seconds the demo says whether it can be recognised, because a plain or glossy surface cannot.
A face, 478 points of itThe face surface is tracked in real time and worn items sit on it, following expression and angle. Eyewear and cosmetics try-on run on exactly this, and so do event filters — the same tracking, two different businesses.
Your own object, cut from the viewFrame a real object, cut it out of the camera image, wipe the background away and set its actual width. It stands in the room as a card that always turns to face you, at the size you gave it, with a shadow under it. Not a reconstruction — a photograph standing up, which is enough to judge whether something fits.
And a capture button on all fourThe camera view and the 3D are saved as one image. Without it a visitor tries something and closes the tab; with it they leave holding the result, which is what makes a campaign measurable.

Nothing leaves the device. Frames are processed in the browser and the target you register never reaches a server. What the demo does send is measurement — frame rate, device model, which demo was opened — so the operator can see where it runs badly.

Limits

What this does not claim

Five of them, and each one shapes what can be planned on top.

It forgets where things werePlacement lasts for the session. Close the tab and the room is unknown again — yesterday's spot cannot be found, because the service that anchored a place to a map was retired with the engine. Printed matter is the way around it: the paper carries the coordinates.
It does not find the floorThe engine returns scattered feature points, not surfaces. On the two phones measured here a plane was never detected once, so the floor height is assumed from how high the phone is held. Objects share one ground and stay consistent with each other, but a placement is approximate, not surveyed.
A preview, not a photographThe browser receives a preview stream, not the multi-frame processing a camera app applies to a picture. The image is softer than the one the phone's own camera produces, and no engine changes that — it is where the web ends.
Old phones run it slowlyA 2026 iPhone holds sixty frames a second; a 2019 Android holds seven. The demo measures this and says so on screen rather than letting it read as a fault, and the heavier demos can be switched off for such devices from the admin page.
AR itself cannot be the productThe engine licence allows free use, including commercial use, but not a paid service whose value comes mostly from the engine. AR has to be the method — the value has to come from the content, the venue or the transaction that surrounds it.
